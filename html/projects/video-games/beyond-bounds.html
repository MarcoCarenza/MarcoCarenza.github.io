Beyond Bounds

Platform: Board Game
Duration: 4 months
Team size: 4 students, 2 supervising professors
Role: Project Lead & Game Designer

All screenshots and videos reflect the work of the entire team (art, code, audio, design…)

OVERVIEW

Created in cooperation with CERN IdeaSquare, with who we developed a physical board game, and tested it in Geneva.

My duties included:

  • Designing various game concepts that explore the i2p cycle while remaining scientifically accurate
  • Design and develop a board game concept, later recreating it in the game Tabletop Simulator
  • Lead team of students and CERN Staff in design meetings. Ensuring that the game remained on track
  • Balancing & playtesting to ensure variance in gameplay

What is the game?

Beyond Bounds is a cooperative game where you are part of a team that is sent on a journey based on the IdeaSquare Planet (i2p) cycle that also matches with CERN’s DNA of staying true to science and reality. The i2p cycle can be explained through the following image, with the journey back to planet earth being included in the game.

The game revolved around managing your supplies so that you and your crew (the players) could survive the space journey and eventually create a sustainable environment on the alien planet. A key aspect of the game was the crew dealing with moral conundrums known as “random events” where they would be presented with ethical dilemmas forcing them to make a difficult decision on how to proceed.

As an example Upon landing on the new planet. You need to place special sensors outside to measure the levels of radiation, which is necessary to ensure the safety of the whole crew throughout the mission. Two people volunteered to go out on this dangerous expedition to place the first sensor. A 50 year old and an inexperienced but ambitious 20 year old. Who do you choose to send, knowing that the older volunteer will need medical attention afterwards while the younger volunteer could make it without harm but there is also the chance for much darker consequences. These events were based on thought experiments such as the trolley problem, with each decision affecting the rest of their journey.

I was responsible for leading the project, concepting, designing, and playtesting the project, writing out all of the random events, and sketching out various versions of game pieces and the game board.

How to play

The goal of the game is to successfully complete the i2p cycle, and having enough resources to reach the end of the game. The players need to manage their resources (water, food, medical, energy), complete tasks in order to gain resources, and collaborate towards resolving the Event Cards to achieve the best possible outcome. If the water supply is less than 0 the game is over.

To start the game, each player needs to choose a Hero they want to play as, as each character has different stats. An example would be Energy Efficient, every time the player completes a task, they gain an additional energy supply. These stats will be used in skill checks, with a skill check occurring through Event Cards or Task Cards. A Check is a challenge for a player to succeed in something. In order to succeed, the player who pulls the card will need to roll a d10 and roll higher than the number required. Once the players reach Phase 4 (the main part of the game), the player will have a time tracker that marks the passage of time.

A turn starts with the player having to decide which supply they want to work on. They then pull a task card. Once they receive their task, they may choose a crew member (who is not in the medical bay) to help them with the task. If the task is completed successfully, (you roll high enough) the supplies of the respective team are earned, if failed (you dont roll high enough) you gain no supplies.

Leading the project

When starting the project I was selected as one of the 4 head students to steer the project and shape it into what it was going to be. Initially, we were supposed to make a video game and had written out a design document for a real time farming simulation, set on an exoplanet. However, after reevaluating the team's skill and available time for the project, we pivoted away and tried designing a cooperative exploration puzzle game. Unfortunately, with even less time we realized that multiplayer wasn't achieveable so we began looking for new ideas.

In the end, I came up with the idea to make a board game that could then be recreated digitally. I then took a weekend to work on nothing but the design and rules for the game and presented it to the team. We agreed that this was going to be the direction of the project, and we quickly iterated on the idea before bringing it to CERN in Geneva. While working on it we were able to make a functioning digital version in Tabletop Simulator, so that we could playtest the game remotely. Once on site in Geneva we would spend the week there developing, playtesting, and iterating on the game we brought.

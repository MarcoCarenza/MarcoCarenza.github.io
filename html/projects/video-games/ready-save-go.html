OVERVIEW

Ready? Save, Go! is an entry for the Coolmath Game Jam 2026, with the objective to teach players about financial literacy concepts and how to use money wisely.

You just moved out from your parents’ place and you want to learn to become independent. For the upcoming year you must manage your money, and have at least 5000$ in your savings while being as happy as possible to see if you can make it on your own!

My duties included:

  • Designing gameplay features (minigames, random events, optional lessons...)
  • Balancing & playtesting to ensure optimal gameflow (menus, scoreboard, lobby, HUD…)
  • Asset integration (2D sprites, animations, music and SFX…)
  • Collaborating closely with the team to ensure the development follows the design and the game can be easily fine-tuned and balanced

Minigame Mockups & Random events

A minigame Mockups was originally designed on Canva in order to visually communicate what each minigame is, how it should work, and all possible player interactions. Each minigame is supposed to be an intuitive game that teaches the player an aspect of financial responsibility. As an example the shopping cart mini game is supposed to teach the player to look at the price per lb on an object and not just the flat price to know which of the two food items is the best deal.

Each random event is a decision for each player to make about how they want to balance their resources. These events are designed to give the player a choice on whether or not they want to increase or decrease, their savings or happiness. This is meant to teach the player that sometimes you need to make sacrificies to your happiness in order to hit your savings goals. Each random event has a button that dives deeper into the topic explaining financial literacy concepts.

GAMEPLAY

The goal of the game is to meet your savings goal at the end of the year, while trying to maximize your happiness. The gameplay loop of the game is simple, the player has minigames where the player must do an activity that involves spending or making money, which is followed by a random event that prompts the player to do an action which would affect their money or happiness. The random events are there to break up the monotony of the game. This cycle is rinsed and repeated until your year is over. The overall score at the end is calculated by taking the score and multiplying it by the happiness.

OVERVIEW

An intership for designing the levels of a daily trivia game called TheList developed by the New York based studio LocalNo.12! TThe game is meant to test the players' knowledge of pop culture and other topics! Done while I was starting the second year of my MFA program.

Initially the internship was supposed to only last a month, but my employers were happy with our results and extended it for both interns by a few months.

My duties included:

  • Research & Fact check various topics in order to create levels and later implement them
  • Maintain a spreadsheet that analyzes data and helps with list creation
  • Implement all levels in the in-house engine
  • Collaborating closely with the full time team to ensure the development follows the design vision

What I did

I was tasked with researching, developing, playtesting, implementing and finalizing levels, where I managed to implement around 60+ levels. Each level consists of 3 lists escalating in size (a list of 5, a list of 7, and a list of 10) and an overarching theme to connect all of the lists together.

While working on the project I had found that we had no efficient check for any similar or duplicate topics for all of the lists, nor a way to keep track of how many topics we covered with each list.

In response to this I took the initiative to make an excel sheet and transfer over the data from the in-house game engine, and then manually assign different categories for each list. The excel sheet allowed us keep track of the distribution of categories, as well as adding functionality to search the different lists and ensure each idea was unique. This excel sheet was then used to implement all the list data back into the in-house engine we were using for the project.

Topic selection

The process for selecting the different themes would vary from week to week. Initially, I would just think up of any interesting topic that is important to pop culture. However, overtime as we covered more and more topics I decided to have brainstorm sessions and think of more obscure things to write about. Sometimes the overall theme would come first, and then the list, other times the 3 lists came first and I formed a theme around it.

I would generally have a topic I wanted to explore, then decide how well known of a topic it would be and how many options there are. For example in the Musicians list, it started with everyone knowing the artist formally known as Prince being short, but figured that not many musicians heights are known, so it could only be a 5 item list. The 10 item list is something everyone would know, with many options, like most streamed artists. Everyone can try and sort who they think is most popular. After a while it was difficult to know what topics we had covered and what we hadn't, which is when I decided to make the excel sheet, and add all the categories. From here I could start seeing trends in our levels and find under used topics.

Research

For every list we had to verify that the information we used was realiable, because otherwise the player could feel cheated. Additionally, if we were just making things up, it would ruin the whole point of the game. I would always try to take information from reliable well known institutions, as we have to write our sources in each list.
